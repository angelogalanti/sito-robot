Go2: Air, Pro, X or EDU?

Sources consulted: 2026-10-23. Document-based comparison, hardware not tested by us.

For exploring robotics, consider Air and Pro. For development, confirm the access and equipment supplied with EDU. Clarify the scope of software support for X: do not assume it is equivalent to EDU.

More than a speed difference

Air, Pro, X and EDU are not the same purchase with a faster motor. Features, accessories and development access differ. This table reports Unitree’s statements, not our test results.

Feature Air Pro X EDU
Graphical programming Listed Listed Listed Listed
ISS 2.0 person following Not listed as included Listed Listed Listed
Official advanced development Not listed Not listed Intermediate support; clarify scope Listed; confirm configuration
Stated runtime 1–2 hours 1–2 hours 1–2 hours 2–4 hours for the listed configuration

Two meanings of programming

Unitree lists graphical programming on every version. Secondary development is not listed for Air and Pro, has intermediate support for X and is listed for EDU. A graphical sequence is not full access to motors, sensors and algorithms. Unofficial integrations do not guarantee support or future compatibility.

Payload and runtime are not completed tasks

The matrix states around 1–2 hours for Air, Pro and X, and 2–4 hours for the displayed EDU configuration. These are not guaranteed working hours in your environment. A payload on its back does not prove it can load or unload objects itself. Charging dock compatibility does not mean the dock is included.

Compare like-for-like prices

The international matrix lists Air at USD 1,600, Pro at USD 2,800 and X at USD 4,500, excluding taxes and freight; EDU requires a quote. Compare the same configuration and package with a European offer, not a currency conversion of a headline price.

A discrepancy to resolve

Source: manufacturer comparison matrix, consulted on 23 October 2026. These are not our measurements. An Italian seller describes person following on Air, whereas Unitree does not list ISS 2.0 for Air: confirm the exact feature before ordering.

What two independent tests say

FauxHammer spent extended time with a Go2 Pro (review updated September 2026): one of the most interesting consumer tech products tested, and one of the least essential. Summary: buy it for fun, novelty and robotics curiosity; do not buy it expecting genuine utility.4

New Atlas, in its Go2 Pro test, calls it “kind of a tool and kind of a toy”: demanding startup (detailed instructions only in the app’s videos), falls possible at power-on on unsuitable surfaces, 16 kg with powerful motors that can scratch, knees wearing on concrete. Observed price: USD 2,800 plus USD 399–1,000 shipping.5

Among r/unitree owners, concrete issues appear: LiDAR stopping after about 40 minutes (one reported case), advanced mode activation unavailable, questions about flying with the battery. These are useful reports, not reliability statistics.67

Get these confirmed before paying

  • Exact version, battery, controller and accessories included.
  • The required feature demonstrated on that configuration, with any operator disclosed.
  • Official software access and development work required.
  • Delivered cost for your country and written support conditions.
