> Before comparing two prices, establish **who sells you the robot, where it ships from and whether you are buying as a consumer or for business**. These answers affect costs, protection and fault handling. This guide uses EU institutional sources: it is not a customs quotation or a legal assessment of an individual offer.[2](#sources)[3](#sources)[6](#sources)

## 1. The seller and the warehouse are different things

An English-language page, euro pricing or an “EU warehouse” label is not enough for our checks. Request the contracting seller's legal identity, address, dispatch country and repair location: four different pieces of information.

| Situation | What to verify | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Goods already in the EU at the time of sale | Confirm they are not still subject to import clearance and check the total price | For the consumer purchases described by the Commission, goods already in the EU require no customs formalities and VAT is included in the final price.[2](#sources) |
| Shipment from outside the EU, even through a European website | Who imports, declares and pays taxes, any duties and customs handling | The parcel may involve import formalities and charges: a European domain does not remove them.[2](#sources) |
| Non-EU trader targeting EU consumers | Contract, applicable protection and practical enforcement | EU consumer rules may apply, but enforcing rights against a non-EU trader can be difficult.[6](#sources) |

This guide refers to **the European Union**, not every geographically European country. Your destination and territories with special arrangements require a specific check.[2](#sources)

## 2. Request the delivered cost, not just the list price

For imports, the Commission distinguishes import VAT, any customs duties and clearance fees. The VAT taxable amount can include customs value, duties and charges, shipping and insurance: applying a percentage only to the robot's price is not enough.[2](#sources)

We do not assign a generic customs rate to “humanoids”: ask the clearance provider to confirm classification, origin and treatment of your configuration. Tariffs depend on the type of goods and can depend on origin.[2](#sources)

| Item to document | Confirmation to request |
| --- | --- |
| Hardware | Exact version, hands, computing, sensors, batteries, charger and controller |
| Software and commissioning | Included or excluded SDK access, licences, cloud services, training and development |
| Delivery | Shipping, insurance, dispatch location and promised date |
| Importing | Who submits the declaration, any duties, VAT and courier fees |
| Total | Currency, destination and amounts still payable on delivery |

Do not count included charges twice: ask which are included and who will settle them. For a business, do not automatically assume VAT is deductible or the transaction has the same treatment as a private purchase: have your adviser check the invoice and transaction.

## 3. Consumer purchases and business purchases

**In Italy, the purpose of the purchase matters.** The EU goods-sale directive defines a consumer as a natural person acting outside their trade or profession; the consumer regime described concerns contracts between consumers and professional traders. A VAT number alone does not settle every case.[3](#sources) This EU definition does not replace checking the applicable national rules.

| Purchase | What not to confuse |
| --- | --- |
| Consumer buying from a professional trader | For new goods, the EU legal guarantee for lack of conformity is at least **two years from delivery**; a shorter commercial warranty cannot reduce applicable consumer protection.[1](#sources)[6](#sources) |
| Company or professional buying for work | Do not automatically transfer consumer rules to B2B. Check applicable law, contract, notification deadlines, remedies and support; this does not mean “no rights”. The goods-sale directive concerns contracts between consumers and sellers.[3](#sources) |
| Purchase from another private individual | The EU consumer protection described here does not cover private-to-private sales: check applicable terms and rights separately.[6](#sources) |

For consumers, Your Europe identifies the **seller** as the legal guarantee contact: distinguish a manufacturer's voluntary support from the seller's obligations. Remedies include free repair or replacement and, in the circumstances provided, price reduction or contract termination: a fault does not always mean an immediate refund.[1](#sources) Your Europe describes the EU minimum protection and directs buyers to national rules.[1](#sources)

## 4. Withdrawal and faulty goods are different issues

For consumer distance contracts covered by EU rules, withdrawal is generally available for **14 days from delivery of the goods**. This is not an unconditional free trial: exceptions include made-to-order or clearly personalised goods. Do not assume every accessory selection automatically excludes withdrawal for the robot: ask how the configuration and any claimed exception are assessed.[4](#sources)[6](#sources)

For a change of mind, the consumer normally pays return costs unless the seller agrees to cover them or failed to disclose them before purchase. A defective product is different: Your Europe assigns the cost of returning defective goods to the trader.[4](#sources)

**Our practical precaution:** before paying, request the return address, a transport quotation including batteries, required packaging and collection procedure. Do not treat a generic “returns available” statement as a cost estimate or confirmation of an EU repair centre.

## 5. Support, spare parts and conformity

A guarantee, development support and maintenance are not interchangeable. Our commercial checklist:

- Who helps with first use, and in which language? Is this training, SDK support or fault handling only?
- Where are repairs performed? Who arranges collection and shipping, including batteries?
- Which spares can be ordered, at what prices and lead times? We do not assume local availability.
- Which modifications, falls and uses are excluded from commercial coverage? Such exclusions do not, by themselves, determine mandatory consumer rights.[1](#sources)
- Which functions remain available without cloud access or after a service expires?

For documentation, a CE logo on a shop page is not enough. CE marking is the manufacturer's declaration of conformity with applicable rules, not permission issued by a central EU office. The procedure depends on the product and relevant legislation.[5](#sources) **We therefore request documentation matching the actual model and configuration**, instructions and operating limits; we have not validated the cited robots' conformity or suitability for working near customers or employees. Business deployment also needs a context-specific assessment.

## 6. Before paying: six written answers

1. Who contracts with me, and where does the robot physically ship from?
2. What is the configuration code, and what task has been demonstrated on that version?
3. What is the total for my destination, including any charges payable on arrival?
4. Which terms apply to my consumer or business purchase?
5. Who handles faults, where does it ship for repair, and what would a change-of-mind return cost?
6. Which software access, documents and spare parts will I receive or be able to obtain?

Keep the offer, terms, payment record, invoice and feature confirmations. For an EU cross-border consumer dispute, Your Europe directs buyers to their national European Consumer Centre.[1](#sources)[6](#sources)
